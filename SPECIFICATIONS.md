# The Corbin Falls Gazette — Feature Specifications

This document describes the current in-repo feature set, reconciled against the
source. Line-number citations were dropped when the files they pointed into
were rewritten; a claim here is checked by reading the element it names, not by
following a line number that has since moved.

## Shape of the site
- **The front page (`index.html`)** is one issue: lead item with deck and
  byline, a briefs column, corrections, weather, classifieds, letters, and a
  colophon. A complete standing issue is present in the markup, so the page
  reads without JavaScript.
- **Issue composition (`modules/issue.js`)** is deterministic. The issue number
  is days elapsed since the first edition (2024-09-29); that number seeds
  selection from `data/formats.js`. `/?issue=YYYY-MM-DD` renders any past
  issue, and `archive.html` lists recent ones.
- **Landmark editions (`data/editions.js`)** are written rather than dealt:
  the first edition, the hundredth, No. 288, the anniversaries. Each has its
  own lead, so the corrections chain runs through it; some carry an
  editorial. The entry cites them by number and links to them.
- **The calendar (`data/calendar.js`, `modules/calendar.js`)** runs dated
  items — the council's Tuesday, the clocks, his birthday, Thanksgiving —
  in place of a dealt item on the days they happen, and every lead carries a
  follow-up brief that runs the next morning.
- **The page has no interactive half.** It loads seven scripts, none of
  which handle input, and ends on the colophon. The only URL parameter is
  `?issue=`.

## Page-wide expectations
### Document metadata
- `<html lang="en">` is set, the front-page title is **"The Corbin Falls Gazette"** in the markup and **"The Corbin Falls Gazette · No. n"** once the issue renders, and every page carries a non-empty meta description.
- Open Graph tags are provided for title, description, image, and URL in the static HTML head; `og:image` references `og-image.png` (1200×630 PNG, generated by `bun run og:generate`) as the standing card. At the edge the middleware replaces it with the per-issue card (see *Edge functions*).
- Every page links the icon set (`/favicon.ico`, `/favicon.svg`, `/apple-touch-icon.png`), the web manifest, and the feed (`/feed.xml`) as `rel="alternate"`.

### Fonts & base theming
- No font request leaves the site. Fraunces is self-hosted as a variable woff2
  (`fonts/fraunces-latin-var.woff2`, `font-display: swap`) and used through
  `--headline-font`; body copy uses the system stack in `--font`.
- CSS root variables define the newsprint palette, radius and shadow; `body` uses the `--bg`/`--text` aliases over `--surface-1`/`--text-primary`. A `prefers-color-scheme: dark` block redefines the tokens.
- The palette is newsprint, ink and one second colour: groundwood stock at `--surface-2` on a near-black ground at `--surface-1`, warm near-black ink at `--text-primary`, and `--ink-display` aliased to that same ink so the nameplate and headlines print in it. `--accent` is a ledger teal, carried by the cutoff rule under the masthead (`--accent-rule`, a step down from the links in the dark theme so a 3px line does not glow) and by links. In the dark theme the ground sits a full step under the plate (`--sheet-edge` gives the plate a faint outline) and `--font-smoothing` switches to greyscale antialiasing.
- Fraunces is used for the lead, page titles, the briefs, the deck, the letters, the editorial's head, the archive's leads, and the name on the folio line. The optical-size axis picks the cut for each size; the subset carries no italic, so nothing set in it relies on `<em>`.

### Layout grid & spacing
- Content is wrapped in `.page-shell` (max width 1180px), square-cornered, with a tight shadow and no border in the light theme — a sheet on a desk. At ≤600px the sheet fills the viewport with no frame, and the desk shows only past its foot. Every page is a single column; the front page goes to the broadsheet grid at ≥760px, and the entry and standards hang their heads in a side column at ≥900px. Body padding is 28/24/48 px on the desk.
- One radius, `--radius: 2px`, on the skip link.

## Masthead
Every page opens on one folio line, closed by the cutoff rule.

- The line carries three things: the paper — `.masthead__kicker` **Corbin
  Falls** at 0.74rem small caps and `.masthead__name` **The Gazette** in
  Fraunces at 1.04rem — then the date, then the site links. The paper is
  named for its town, not its subject; "Neckass" appears in the copy as a
  surname would, which is the point. The name is set at the size of a credit
  rather than a banner: the reader arrived at neckass.com and does not need
  telling twice whose page this is, and the lead should be the first thing on
  the page set large. It is the one thing on the line in the display face,
  which tells the eye which face the lead below is in.
- The line closes with a 3px rule in the accent. That rule is the paper's
  cutoff: everything below it is the news.
- `#masthead-date` ships with standing copy — "Published daily · Corbin Falls"
  — so the page reads before, and without, JavaScript, and is replaced on load
  with `<date> · Vol. <roman>, No. <n>`, the date and the volume each in a
  `span` with the middot in `.masthead__sep` between them. Neither span
  breaks inside itself; on a phone they take a line each and the middot is
  hidden. Volumes turn over on the anniversary of the first edition. Pages
  that are not an issue carry the links alone.
- Under 1060px the folio line will not hold all three parts, so the name
  and the site links share the first row and the date takes the second, on
  its own, directly over the cutoff rule.
- Site links are text, not buttons, and the current page is marked with
  `aria-current="page"`, which underlines it in the accent.
- `#back-issue-notice` is `hidden` on today's issue. When `?issue=YYYY-MM-DD`
  resolves to a past date it is revealed, names the issue being read, and
  offers a link back to today. A date past today is an issue that has not
  gone to press: the reader gets today's paper and the notice says when the
  one they asked for is due, without the link.

## Rules, labels and cuts
The page has one vocabulary of horizontal rules, and it means something:

- **3px solid** — the masthead cutoff. It is the only rule in the accent; a
  `.page-title` prints no rule of its own, since the masthead's is a hundred
  pixels above it.
- **2px solid** — a department head (`.section-label`): In brief, Notices,
  Weather, Classifieds, Letters. The rule runs *above* the head, with the
  head close under it and the copy close under that, so each department
  starts on one line and the heads of two columns side by side draw one rule
  across the fold. The lead and the lower row carry no rules of their own;
  the heads' rules are the divisions.
- **1px `--border-strong`** — a division inside a department, the colophon.
- **1px `--border`** — between items in a list, and the column rules.

Line-cuts live in `assets/cuts/` and are rigs: each exposes its moving parts
as CSS custom properties, with fallbacks, so the files work as plain `<img>`
and pose when inlined. `assets/cuts/README.md` lists the properties.

- **The file cut** — `assets/cuts/neckass.svg` with a cutline, at the foot of
  the briefs column. The briefs run shorter than the notices beside them; the
  cut is what a paper puts in that hole.
- **Reading-page side heads** — at ≥900px, a `.seo-copy` that has
  `.section-label` children (the entry, the standards) becomes a 200px / 68ch
  grid: the title and copy in the text column, each head in the margin with
  its rule level with the top of the section's first paragraph. The entry's
  `.entry-note` sits in the margin beside the opening paragraph, spanning two
  rows; its place in the markup, after the intro, is unchanged.
- **The weather cut** — the same drawing as `assets/cuts/weather.svg`, inlined
  in the weather box so its layers can be switched. `frontPage.weatherLayers()`
  maps the first word of the forecast to `--sun`, `--cloud`, `--rain` and
  `--wind`; anything it does not recognise gets sun and cloud.
- **The town map** — `assets/cuts/map.svg`, inlined as the last department
  before the colophon (`.box--map`, head *The town*). Each place on it has a
  ring; `frontPage.placesNamed()` scans the day's notices and letters and
  sets `--bridge`, `--orchard`, `--station`, `--depot`, `--hall`,
  `--library`, `--center`, `--gym`, `--school`, `--route9`, `--river` and
  `--shelter` on the svg, and the caption names what is ringed. `--bridge`
  also reduces the bridge to one lane.
- **The press** — `assets/cuts/press.svg`, on the 404 page.

## The issue (front page)
- **Lead** — `#lead-headline`, `#lead-deck`, `#lead-byline`. At ≥760px the
  lead sits on the same two columns as the departments below it: the headline
  in the wide one, against the column rule, and the deck and byline in the
  narrow one. The deck is set in Fraunces at text size, one step under the
  headline, and opens on a wire dateline, `CORBIN FALLS, Sept. 5 —`, in AP
  month style. Bylines are staff names from the colophon, or "Staff report" /
  "From wire reports".
- **Lead selection** — leads are dealt in rounds: the pool is shuffled once
  per round of its own length, seeded by the round, and issue *n* takes its
  place in the round, so no lead runs twice inside a round. A landmark
  (`data.EDITIONS[n].lead`) is taken instead of the dealt one.
- **Editorial** — `#editorial`, `hidden` unless the issue is a landmark with
  one: a head, paragraphs, and the editor's signature, under the lead and
  across the page, in two columns at ≥760px.
- **Briefs** — `#briefs-list`, seven items. The first is the *previous*
  issue's lead's `followUp` — he has done the next thing — then any calendar
  briefs for the date, then items dealt from `data.HEADLINES`, minus anything
  already running as the lead. The earliest issues are shorter: the column
  grows from three to seven over the first year (`briefsCount`).
- **Notices** — `#notices-list`, three entries: any calendar notices for the
  date (the council meets the first and third Tuesday; holiday collection
  changes; the paper's anniversary), then entries from `data.NOTICES`: leaf
  collection, lane closures, the flu clinic. None mention him. They are the
  furniture that makes the column look like a paper's.
- **Corrections** — `#corrections-list`, four entries. The first is a routine
  correction from `data.ROUTINE_CORRECTIONS` (a misidentified street, a wrong
  vote count) and never mentions him. The second retracts a detail of the
  *previous* issue's actual lead, from that lead's `correction` field. Then
  any calendar correction, then the rest from `data.CORRECTIONS`. Issue 1 is
  written and runs one correction, which says there is nothing to correct.
- **Weather** — `#weather-numbers` carries a seeded, seasonally plausible
  forecast (`High 71°, low 55°. Winds SW 8 mph. Sunset 7:14 p.m.`); the
  forecaster's note about him follows in `#weather-line`.
- **Classifieds** — `#classifieds-list`, five entries. The first is the lead's own (`lead.classified`), then any calendar ad, then the rest from `data.CLASSIFIEDS`.
- **Letters** — `#letters-list`, two entries (one for the first sixty issues), each a body and a signature. The first is from the person the lead happened to (`lead.letter`), then any calendar letter (his mother writes on his birthday), then one dealt from `data.LETTERS`. Every lead carries a deck, a correction, a follow-up, a classified and a letter, so the lead, its ad, and its letter always run in the same issue and its follow-up and retraction run the next. The body is set in Fraunces: it is the one place on the page where someone other than the paper is speaking.
- **The town** — the map, see *Rules, labels and cuts*.
- **Colophon** — the staff line, the paper's note, and links to the back
  issues, the issue's clipping (`#colophon-card`, `/card.png?issue=…`) and
  the entry.
- Calendar items take the place of dealt ones, so no section changes size
  on a dated day. Pool depth is load-bearing: each pool must stay several
  times its deal size or a reader returning on consecutive days sees the
  same paper.
- **Print** — `@media print` sets ink on white, drops the desk, the shadow,
  the site links, the back-issue notice and the clipping link, and keeps the
  broadsheet's columns whatever the sheet's width (the ≥760px queries also
  match print).

## Back issues (`archive.html`)
- Lists every issue newest first, by month (`#archive-run`, a
  `.archive-month` per month with a `.section-label` head), each row a link
  to `/?issue=YYYY-MM-DD` carrying the folio (`No. n · Weekday d`) and the
  issue's lead in Fraunces. The current issue and the landmarks carry an
  `.archive-tag` under the lead.
- The landmark editions are listed first under *Of note*
  (`#archive-landmarks`), oldest first.
- The list is derived from the calendar, not stored, and stops at issue 1;
  `issue.recentIssues()` returns the whole run with each lead and tag.
- Rows take the ink; the lead underlines in the accent on hover. Two
  columns at ≥760px, one on a phone.
- Without JavaScript the page still explains the archive and links to today.

## Edge functions (Cloudflare Pages)
- `functions/feed.xml.js` serves `/feed.xml`: RSS for the last 30 issues,
  composed per request, `cache-control: public, max-age=3600`. Item titles and
  descriptions are XML-escaped.
- `functions/card.png.js` serves `/card.png?issue=YYYY-MM-DD` (today's if
  none): the issue's lead on the sheet, 1200×630 PNG, drawn by Satori and
  resvg (`workers-og`) with two static cuts of Fraunces fetched from
  `/fonts/` through the ASSETS binding. `cache-control: public,
  max-age=86400`; 404 before the first edition.
- `functions/_middleware.js` rewrites `<title>`, the `og:`/`twitter:` title
  and description, `og:image`/`og:image:secure_url`/`twitter:image` (to the
  card for the same issue) and the image alt tags, on the front page only,
  so a shared link previews with the headline actually running in the text
  and in the picture.
- All three compose the lead through `functions/lib/issue.mjs`, which
  mirrors the arithmetic in `modules/issue.js`, including the landmark
  leads. The leads are extracted to `functions/lib/leads.mjs` by `bun run
  feed:build`; `bun run feed:check` fails on drift and a smoke test asserts
  both runtimes select the same lead. The smoke suite runs the functions
  under `wrangler pages dev` (`bun run edge`, port 8800) and checks the card
  is a real PNG and the tags point at it.

## Responsive behavior checkpoints
The paper reads in one column, two, or the broadsheet grid, in that order:

- **≥900px**: the entry and the standards hang their heads in a 200px side
  column.
- **≥760px**: the broadsheet grid — the lead and the departments on
  1.55fr / 1fr against a column rule, the editorial in two columns, the map
  beside its caption, and the archive in two columns.
- **560–759px**: the departments pair up two across (notices with corrections,
  classifieds with letters) with the weather spanning both, rather than running
  down the page one after another. Everything else is one column.
- **≥560px**: the file cut and its cutline share a line.
- **<560px**: one column throughout; the cutline sits under the cut.
- **≤600px**: the sheet fills the viewport with no frame or border, the date
  and the volume take a line each on the folio, and the headline text size
  is reduced.

## Wrapping
- Display type balances (`text-wrap: balance`): the lead headline and page
  titles.
- Running copy avoids the one-word last line (`text-wrap: pretty`): briefs,
  notices, corrections, classifieds, letters, decks, the colophon, cutlines,
  the weather, and the long-form pages.
- The folio line puts the date and the site links on one line where they fit
  and stacks them from the same margin where they do not.

## Accessibility checklist
- Every link has discernible text; every department is a labelled region.
- The inline cuts are `aria-hidden`; the map's places are named in its
  caption, which is the accessible account of what is ringed.
- Visible focus outlines are preserved for keyboard navigation
  (`a:focus-visible`).
