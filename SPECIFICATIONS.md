# The Gazette — Feature Specifications

This document describes the current in-repo feature set, reconciled against the
source. Line-number citations were dropped when the files they pointed into
were rewritten; a claim here is checked by reading the element it names, not by
following a line number that has since moved.

## Shape of the site
- **The front page (`index.html`)** is one issue: lead item with deck and
  byline, a briefs column, corrections, weather, classifieds, letters, and a
  colophon. A complete standing issue is present in the markup, so the page
  reads without JavaScript.
- **Issue composition (`modules/issue.js`)** is deterministic. The issue number
  is days elapsed since the first edition (2024-09-29); that number seeds
  selection from `data/formats.js`. `/?issue=YYYY-MM-DD` renders any past
  issue, and `archive.html` lists recent ones.
- **Landmark editions (`data/editions.js`)** are written rather than dealt:
  the first edition, the hundredth, No. 288, the anniversaries. Each has its
  own lead, so the corrections chain runs through it; some carry an
  editorial. The entry cites them by number and links to them.
- **The calendar (`data/calendar.js`, `modules/calendar.js`)** runs dated
  items — the council's Tuesday, the clocks, his birthday, Thanksgiving —
  in place of a dealt item on the days they happen, and every lead carries a
  follow-up brief that runs the next morning. A rule may carry
  `from: 'YYYY-MM-DD'` and does not run before it. The standing matters
  are serials: witnesses with open files (the package, the $35, the
  hoodie, the bin) file monthly on the day of the month the grudge began,
  the counts in their items computed from the issue's date out of
  `data.FEUDS`, so a back issue carries the number as of that issue and
  the archive is the ledger; the day after next door's bin letter the
  paper prints a standing correction confirming one fact and declining to
  check the wind. His mother files on Mother's Day and counts her reading
  in issues.
- **The page has no interactive half.** It loads seven scripts, none of
  which handle input, and ends on the colophon. The only URL parameter is
  `?issue=`.

## Page-wide expectations
### Document metadata
- `<html lang="en">` is set, the front-page title is **"The Gazette"** in the markup and **"The Gazette · No. n"** once the issue renders, and every page carries a non-empty meta description.
- Open Graph tags are provided for title, description, image, and URL in the static HTML head; `og:image` references `og-image.png` (1200×630 PNG, generated by `bun run og:generate`) as the standing card. At the edge the middleware replaces it with the per-issue card (see *Edge functions*).
- Every page links the icon set (`/favicon.ico`, `/favicon.svg`, `/apple-touch-icon.png`), the web manifest, and the feed (`/feed.xml`) as `rel="alternate"`.
- The rendered front page and the edge response carry an `Article` schema for the selected lead, with `genre: Satire`, its dated permalink, and its issue card. The fictional byline and issue date do not supply author identities or publication timestamps. Back-issue canonical and share URLs retain `?issue=YYYY-MM-DD`; future requests describe today's issue, as the browser displays it.

### Fonts & base theming
- No font request leaves the site. Fraunces is self-hosted as a variable woff2
  (`fonts/fraunces-latin-var.woff2`, `font-display: swap`) and used through
  `--headline-font`; the two other voices are system stacks.
- The page speaks in two voices. Every sentence of the paper is set in
  Fraunces, from the lead down to an ad. The wire's apparatus is set in the
  system monospace (`--wire-font`), upper case as the teletype was: the
  folio line and the site links, the department heads, bylines, the issue
  folios in *Earlier issues* and the back issues, the forecast's figures,
  an ad's keyword, a letter's signature, the cutline's *FILE*, and the
  colophon. The reference entry is a third publication, an encyclopedia's,
  and its body is set in the system sans (`--reference-font`), which has the
  true italic its mentions and citations need.
- CSS root variables define the newsprint palette, radius and shadow; `body` uses the `--bg`/`--text` aliases over `--surface-1`/`--text-primary`. A `prefers-color-scheme: dark` block redefines the tokens.
- The palette is newsprint, ink and spot colours, each with one job: groundwood stock at `--surface-2` (grey newsprint, not cream) on a near-black ground at `--surface-1`, warm near-black ink at `--text-primary`, and `--ink-display` aliased to that same ink so the nameplate and headlines print in it.
  - `--accent` is a ledger teal, the record. It carries the cutoff rule under the masthead (`--accent-rule`, a step down from the links in the dark theme so a 3px line does not glow), the links, and the issue folios in *Earlier issues* and the back issues.
  - `--ink-rubric` is a rubricator's red, for the corrections and the red-letter issues: the heads of Corrections, the editorial and *Of note* (`.section-label--rubric`), the dash before each correction, and the landmark tags in the back issues.
  - `--ink-sun` and `--ink-sky` are the weather's inks. They fill the sun's disc and the rain in the weather cut and print nowhere else.
  - A screen is an ink at a tint: `--screen-ledger` (18% teal) behind the back-issue notice, and `--screen-sun` behind selected text, as a highlighter.

  Text in the rubric holds 4.5:1 on the stock and on the dark plate. In the dark theme the ground sits a full step under the plate (`--sheet-edge` gives the plate a faint outline) and `--font-smoothing` switches to greyscale antialiasing.
- The optical-size axis picks Fraunces's cut for each size. The subset carries no italic, so nothing set in it relies on `<em>`: the paper sets a word used as a word in quotes, as the wire does, and the motto and the signatures are roman.

### Layout grid & spacing
- Content is wrapped in `.page-shell` (max width 1180px), square-cornered, with a tight shadow and no border in the light theme — a sheet on a desk. At ≤600px the sheet fills the viewport with no frame, and the desk shows only past its foot. Every page is a single column; the front page goes to the broadsheet grid at ≥760px, and the entry and standards hang their heads in a side column at ≥900px. Body padding is 28/24/48 px on the desk.
- One radius, `--radius: 2px`, on the skip link.

## Masthead
Every page opens on one folio line. The inside pages close it with the
cutoff rule; the front page sets the nameplate under it and closes that.

- On the inside pages the line carries three things: the paper —
  `.masthead__name` **The Gazette** in Fraunces at 1.16rem — then the date,
  then the site links. The paper is not named for its subject, and it is
  not placed anywhere; "Neckass" appears in the copy as a surname would,
  which is the point.
- The front page carries the nameplate instead (`.masthead--front`): the
  folio line holds the date and the site links over a hairline, and under
  it the `h1` **The Gazette** is set in Fraunces at up to
  5.6rem, centred, on one line at every width (13.5vw below the cap), the
  largest type on the page, with `.nameplate__motto` — *One man, reported
  daily since Sept. 29, 2024.* — in the roman beneath. The front page is where a reader
  lands from a shared link knowing nothing; the name says whose paper it is
  and the motto says what it covers, in the line before the lead.
- The masthead closes with a thick-and-thin in the accent, a 3px rule with
  a hairline 4px under it — under the folio line on the inside pages, under
  the nameplate on the front. That rule is the paper's cutoff: everything
  below it is the news.
- `#masthead-date` ships with standing copy — "Published daily" — so the page reads before, and without, JavaScript, and is replaced on load
  with `<date> · Vol. <roman>, No. <n>`, the date and the volume each in a
  `span` with the middot in `.masthead__sep` between them. Neither span
  breaks inside itself; on a phone they take a line each and the middot is
  hidden. Volumes turn over on the anniversary of the first edition. Pages
  that are not an issue carry the links alone.
- Under 1060px the inside pages' folio line will not hold all three parts,
  so the name and the site links share the first row and the date takes the
  second, on its own, directly over the cutoff rule.
- Site links are text, not buttons, and the current page is marked with
  `aria-current="page"`, which underlines it in the accent.
- On the inside pages the folio line's plate is a link to `/`, in the
  plate's own inks; the name underlines in the accent on hover. The front
  page is home and its nameplate is not a link.
- `#back-issue-notice` is `hidden` on today's issue. When `?issue=YYYY-MM-DD`
  resolves to a past date it is revealed, names the issue being read, and
  offers a link back to today. It hangs from the cutoff rule on a screen of
  the ledger teal. A date past today is an issue that has not
  gone to press: the reader gets today's paper and the notice says when the
  one they asked for is due, without the link.

## Rules, labels and cuts
The page has one vocabulary of horizontal rules, and it means something:

- **3px solid over a hairline** — the masthead cutoff. It is the only rule in the accent; a
  `.page-title` prints no rule of its own, since the masthead's is a hundred
  pixels above it.
- **2px solid** — a department head (`.section-label`): In brief, Corrections,
  Weather, Classifieds, Letters. The rule runs *above* the head, with the
  head close under it and the copy close under that, so each department
  starts on one line and the heads of two columns side by side draw one rule
  across the fold. The lead and the lower row carry no rules of their own;
  the heads' rules are the divisions. The rubric's departments print the
  head and its rule in `--ink-rubric`; every other head is in the ink.
- **1px `--border-strong`** — a division inside a department, the colophon.
- **1px `--border`** — between items in a list, and the column rules.

Line-cuts live in `assets/cuts/` and are rigs: each exposes its moving parts
as CSS custom properties, with fallbacks, so the files work as plain `<img>`
and pose when inlined. `assets/cuts/README.md` lists the properties.

- **The file cut** — the drawing in `assets/cuts/neckass.svg`, inlined in
  the lead (`#cut-figure`) so it can be posed: the paper's one picture of
  him, posed for the lead and run beside it, where a paper runs the
  photograph. Each lead names a pose from
  `data.POSES` — explaining, in his own defense, pointing it out, asked about
  it, satisfied, on his way out, on the phone, considering it, standing by,
  resting his eyes — and `front-page.js` sets its seven joints as custom
  properties, its alt text as the figure's `aria-label`, and its caption in
  the cutline: *FILE — Neckass, on the phone. The Gazette has one cut of him
  and has not needed a second.* Unposed, the joints fall back to the house
  pose, which is what the standing issue shows. The box keeps a margin on
  three sides for a thrown limb, so a pose never draws over the column rule
  or the cutline. The inks are the page's tokens, so the cut follows the
  theme and the print sheet.
- **The scene** — a lead may also name what its story happens among, from
  `data.SCENES`: a phone, a coffee cup or a hat, which he holds or wears
  and which turn with him; or a couch, a television, a desk, a table, a
  doorway, an open fridge, a flip chart, a signpost, a shelf coming off the
  wall, a wall clock, a bedside alarm, a package, a shoe, the rain, a
  running shower or a smoke alarm, which stand behind him on his ground, in
  the margin the box already keeps. Each is a layer of the same drawing with
  a switch (`--phone: 1`), off by default, so the standing issue and the rig
  as an `<img>` show him on the bare sheet. `front-page.js` sets every
  switch, on or off, from the lead, and the scene finishes the figure's
  `aria-label`: *Line-cut of a man with one hand held to his ear, holding a
  phone, in front of a couch.* The cutline names the pose only.
- **The cut's motion** — once `front-page.js` has set the pose (as
  `data-pose`), the cut acts it out: the raised hand makes its point, he
  nods into the phone, waves on his way out, shrugs, protests, scratches
  his head, and, resting his eyes, nods off and catches himself; standing
  by, he stands by. The rain and the shower fall, the alarm clock rings, the
  smoke alarm chirps. Each swing is a `rotate` added to the joint's pose, so
  the drawing comes to rest exactly as printed. It plays once, after 0.4s,
  and every motion is over by 4.4s, so a page with no controls needs none to
  stop it (WCAG 2.2.2); nothing flashes. Under `prefers-reduced-motion:
  reduce` none of it runs. The card and the print sheet are still.
- **Reading-page side heads** — at ≥900px, a `.seo-copy` that has
  `.section-label` children (the entry, the standards) becomes a 200px / 68ch
  grid: the title and copy in the text column, each head in the margin with
  its rule level with the top of the section's first paragraph. The entry's
  `.entry-note` sits in the margin beside the opening paragraph, spanning two
  rows; its place in the markup, after the intro, is unchanged.
- **The weather cut** — the same drawing as `assets/cuts/weather.svg`, inlined
  in the weather box so its layers can be switched. `frontPage.weatherLayers()`
  maps the first word of the forecast to `--sun`, `--cloud`, `--rain` and
  `--wind`; anything it does not recognise gets sun and cloud. The sun's disc
  is filled with `--ink-sun` and the rain is drawn in `--ink-sky`, under the
  same ink line as the rest of the cut; the cloud keeps the sheet, since it
  covers the sun.
- **The press** — `assets/cuts/press.svg`, on the 404 page.

## The issue (front page)
- **Lead** — `#lead-headline`, `#lead-deck`, `#lead-byline` in
  `.lead__story`; the file cut and *Earlier issues* in `.lead__side`. At
  ≥760px the lead sits on the same two columns as the departments below it:
  the story (headline, deck, byline) in the wide one, against the column
  rule, and the cut over the earlier issues in the narrow one, the cutline
  beside the cut from 1000px and under it below that. On a phone the cut
  follows the byline under a hairline. The deck is set in Fraunces at text
  size, one step under the headline. It carries no dateline: the paper is not placed anywhere. Bylines are staff names from the colophon, or "Staff report" /
  "From wire reports".
- **The headline is the issue's permalink** — `#lead-link`, inside
  `#lead-headline`, points at `/?issue=YYYY-MM-DD` for the issue on the page,
  today's included, since `/` is a different paper tomorrow. It prints in
  ink and underlines in the accent only on hover.
- **Lead selection** — leads are dealt in rounds: the pool is shuffled once
  per round of its own length, seeded by the round, and issue *n* takes its
  place in the round, so no lead runs twice inside a round. Where two
  rounds meet, a lead from the last quarter-pool places (at most 14) of one
  round is moved out of the same number of opening places of the next, so a
  lead never returns within that many days; the tail of a round is never
  moved. A landmark (`data.EDITIONS[n].lead`) is taken instead of the dealt
  one.
- **Earlier issues** — `#earlier-list`, in the lead: the three issues
  before this one, each a link to `/?issue=…` carrying its weekday, number
  and lead. The folio is in the ledger teal, the lead in the ink. At ≥760px it sits under the cut in the lead's narrow
  column; below that it follows the cut. The leads are set in the
  regular cut a step under the deck, as an index rather than as news, so
  they do not outweigh today's lead. The standing markup is a single link
  labelled *Back issues*, which is what issue 1, with nothing earlier, keeps.
- **Editorial** — `#editorial`, `hidden` unless the issue is a landmark with
  one: a head, paragraphs, and the editor's signature, under the lead and
  across the page, in two columns at ≥760px.
- **Briefs** — `#briefs-list`, seven items. The first is the *previous*
  issue's lead's `followUp` — he has done the next thing — then any calendar
  briefs for the date, then items dealt from `data.HEADLINES`, which holds
  no lead's headline: the lead tells that story, and a brief would repeat
  it on another morning. The earliest issues are shorter: the column
  grows from three to seven over the first year (`briefsCount`).
- **Corrections** — `#corrections-list`, four entries. The first retracts a
  detail of the *previous* issue's actual lead, from that lead's `correction`
  field. Then any calendar correction, then the rest from `data.CORRECTIONS`.
  Issue 1 is written and runs one correction, which says there is nothing to
  correct. The retraction ends *See No. n.*, linked to the issue it retracts.
  Corrections are set in the ink; the head, its rule, and the dash before
  each are in the rubric.
- **Weather** — `#weather-numbers` carries a seeded, seasonally plausible
  forecast (`High 71°, low 55°. Winds SW 8 mph. Sunset 7:14 p.m.`); the
  forecaster's note about him follows in `#weather-line`.
- **Classifieds** — `#classifieds-list`, five entries. The first is the lead's own (`lead.classified`), then any calendar ad, then the rest from `data.CLASSIFIEDS`. Each ad's keyword (FOR SALE, LOST, CARD OF THANKS) is marked `.classified__key` and set in the wire's face. From 1000px the ads run in two narrow columns, as a classifieds page runs them.
- **Letters** — `#letters-list`, two entries (one for the first sixty issues), each a body and a signature. The first is from the person the lead happened to (`lead.letter`), then any calendar letter (his mother writes on his birthday and on Mother's Day; next door files her monthly bin letter on the 14th), then one dealt from `data.LETTERS`. A serial letter fills the section's size, so a witness files once per issue. Every lead carries a deck, a correction, a follow-up, a classified and a letter, so the lead, its ad, and its letter always run in the same issue and its follow-up and retraction run the next. The body is set in Fraunces; the signature is attribution, like a byline, and is set in the wire's capitals.
- **Colophon** — the staff line (vacant, unattended, ongoing), the note
  (*Letters may be edited.*) and the issue's clipping (`#colophon-card`,
  `/card.png?issue=…`). The folio line already carries the site links; the
  colophon does not repeat them.
- **Section dealing** — briefs, corrections, classifieds, letters and the
  forecaster's note are dealt in rounds of their pools, like the leads, at a
  fixed number of places a day (`DEALS` in `modules/issue.js`: six, three,
  four, one, one). Each pool is split once into four quarters, and every
  round runs the quarters in order, each reshuffled, so an item does not
  return until three quarters of its pool have run, and a day straddling
  two rounds cannot deal one item twice. A day that needs fewer items
  (a calendar item, a short early issue) skips the rest of its places.
- Calendar items take the place of dealt ones, so no section changes size
  on a dated day. Pool depth is load-bearing: each pool must be at least
  four times its places a day, and the deeper it is the longer an item
  stays away; the smoke test holds every pooled item to more than ten days.
- **Print** — `@media print` sets ink on white, with every spot colour
  printed black and the sun's disc left white; it drops the desk, the shadow,
  the site links, the back-issue notice and the clipping link, and keeps the
  broadsheet's columns whatever the sheet's width (the ≥760px queries also
  match print).

## Back issues (`archive.html`)
- Lists every issue newest first, by month (`#archive-run`, a
  `.archive-month` per month with a `.section-label` head), each row a link
  to `/?issue=YYYY-MM-DD` carrying the folio (`No. n · Weekday d`) and the
  issue's lead in Fraunces, the folio in the wire's face. A month's head is
  set in Fraunces at 1.4rem under the department rule, so a reader
  scrolling a year can find the month. The current issue and the landmarks
  carry an `.archive-tag` under the lead.
- The landmark editions are listed first under *Of note*
  (`#archive-landmarks`), oldest first.
- *By month* (`#archive-months`) follows: a line per year, newest first,
  each month in AP style linking to its head in the run
  (`#month-YYYY-MM`). The months wrap in their own column beside the
  year.
- The list is derived from the calendar, not stored, and stops at issue 1;
  `issue.recentIssues()` returns the whole run with each lead and tag.
- The folio takes the ledger teal and the lead the ink, which underlines in
  the accent on hover. A landmark's tag is in the rubric; the current issue's
  (`.archive-tag--current`) is in the teal, since today is not a red-letter
  day. *Of note* takes the rubric on its head and its bullets. Two columns at
  ≥760px, one on a phone.
- Without JavaScript the page still explains the archive and links to today.

## Edge functions (Cloudflare Worker)
- `functions/feed.xml.js` serves `/feed.xml`: RSS for the last 30 issues,
  composed per request, `cache-control: public, max-age=3600`. Item titles and
  descriptions are XML-escaped.
- `functions/card.png.js` serves `/card.png?issue=YYYY-MM-DD` (today's if
  none): the nameplate, the issue's date and number, the lead and its deck
  beside the file cut of him in the lead's pose, and the motto at the foot,
  1200×630 PNG,
  drawn by Satori and resvg with two static cuts of Fraunces and Bitstream
  Vera Sans Mono (the wire's lines: the date and number, the motto, the
  address) fetched from `/fonts/` through the ASSETS binding. It carries
  the page's newsprint and its thick-and-thin under the name. The layout is
  `functions/lib/card.mjs`, which `bun run og:generate` also draws the still
  cards with (the standing issue, the entry, the back issues, the
  standards, each in a pose of its own); the cut is `functions/lib/cut.mjs`,
  the rig flattened in any pose, its box padded for a thrown limb.
  `cache-control: public, max-age=86400`; 404 before the first edition.
- `functions/_middleware.js` rewrites `<title>`, the `og:`/`twitter:` title
  and description, `og:image`/`og:image:secure_url`/`twitter:image` (to the
  card for the same issue) and the image alt tags, on the front page only,
  so a shared link previews with the headline actually running in the text
  and in the picture.
- All three compose the lead through `functions/lib/issue.mjs`, a thin UTC
  adapter over `modules/issue.js` — the same module the browser runs, so the
  runtimes cannot disagree about a lead. The smoke suite runs the Worker
  under `wrangler dev` (`bun run edge`, port 8800) and checks the card
  is a real PNG and the tags point at it.
- The edge answers as Pages did: the card and the feed match in any case,
  with or without a trailing slash, for GET only; the front page carries no
  ETag; a path with no file is `404.html` with status 404 and `no-store`; a
  page's `.html` or trailing-slash address is a 308 to the extensionless
  one, query string kept; any method but GET or HEAD is a 405.

## Responsive behavior checkpoints
The paper reads in one column, two, or the broadsheet grid, in that order:

- **≥900px**: the entry and the standards hang their heads in a 200px side
  column.
- **≥760px**: the broadsheet grid — the lead and the departments on
  1.55fr / 1fr against a column rule, the editorial in two columns, and the
  archive in two columns.
- **560–759px**: the departments pair up two across (corrections with the
  weather, classifieds with letters) rather than running down the page one
  after another. Everything else is one column.
- **≥560px**: the file cut and its cutline share a line.
- **<560px**: one column throughout; the cutline sits under the cut.
- **≤600px**: the sheet fills the viewport with no frame or border, the date
  and the volume take a line each on the folio, and the headline text size
  is reduced.

## Wrapping
- Display type balances (`text-wrap: balance`): the lead headline and page
  titles.
- Running copy avoids the one-word last line (`text-wrap: pretty`): briefs,
  corrections, classifieds, letters, decks, the colophon, cutlines,
  the weather, and the long-form pages.
- The folio line puts the date and the site links on one line where they fit
  and stacks them from the same margin where they do not.

## Accessibility checklist
- Every link has discernible text; every department is a labelled region.
- The inline cuts are `aria-hidden`; the forecast beside the weather cut is
  the accessible account of the sky.
- Visible focus outlines are preserved for keyboard navigation
  (`a:focus-visible`).
